Dear Editors,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "Interrogation Cannot Identify,
Intervention Can: Computational Indicator Properties of Consciousness Under
Test on Language-Model Agents," for the special issue "Is There More to
Consciousness Than Computation?"

The paper approaches the special issue's question experimentally. We
implemented the computational indicator properties of Butlin, Long and
colleagues for global workspace and attention schema theories on
language-model agents, all preregistered in a public git history, and found:
(1) the computational specifications underdetermine outcomes, since choices
the theories never state (the consumer's capacity, the revision trigger, the
salience function) decided every predicted effect; (2) behavioral
identification of the specified organization fails in a way that
empirically instantiates the unfolding argument's concern, with the two
defensible probe designs ordering a real architecture and its prompted
imitation oppositely; and (3) intervention on the implementation escapes
that regress, with self-reports covarying with a capacity dial the assessor
turns, robust to coached imposters and defeated only by leaking the
schedule. We argue this locates the empirically available middle ground of
the computation-versus-implementation debate: what can be certified from
outside is causal coupling to manipulated implementation, and every test
that worked did so by touching the substrate.

The manuscript is a Research Article. All code, per-trial data,
preregistrations, and a response cache sufficient to replay every number at
zero cost are public. A preprint will be posted to arXiv; we note this per
the journal's preprint policy.
